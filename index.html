The Firm

Three decades before the Bar. One standard of care.

Baath & Associates is a chambers practice built around the work of Bikramjit Singh Baath, former Deputy Advocate General, Punjab, whose thirty years before the Bar span constitutional, criminal, civil and service matters — from trial courts across Punjab and Haryana to the High Court at Chandigarh and the Supreme Court of India.

The firm's philosophy is unfashionably simple: preparation decides cases. Every brief is read in full, every precedent traced to its source, every weakness in a client's position identified in conference — long before an opponent finds it in court.

We would rather tell a client an uncomfortable truth in chambers than an easy one at the hearing. That candour, together with strict confidentiality and a strategy built for each matter rather than borrowed from the last, is what clients stay for.

How We Work

Fig. 01 — The long view

Experience that has seen the argument before.

Thirty years of practice means very few situations arrive at the chambers for the first time. Trial strategy, appellate posture, the temperament of a particular forum — these are judgements formed over decades, applied to your matter on day one.

Fig. 02 — The point of attack

Strategy chosen before the first filing.

A matter can usually be fought on five grounds; it should be fought on the right one. We map the whole dispute — forum, limitation, evidence, the opponent's likely course — and commit to the line that wins, not the one that merely occupies the court's time.

Fig. 03 — The sealed wall

Confidentiality, kept the old-fashioned way.

Instructions, documents and conferences stay inside the chambers. Files are discussed with no one beyond the team on the matter; opinions are given to the client and to no other. Discretion is not a policy here — it is a habit of thirty years.

Fig. 04 — The forum

Courtroom advocacy as a craft.

Written pleadings open the door; the hearing walks through it. Arguments at Baath & Associates are rehearsed, compressed, and led from the strongest proposition — because a judge's attention is the scarcest resource in any courtroom, and it is never wasted.

Selected Work

Outcomes, told the way they were won.

ServiceOrder set aside

Reinstatement, with every year restored.

Challenge
A senior officer dismissed after a disciplinary inquiry conducted, in the firm's assessment, in breach of natural justice.
Approach
Rather than re-arguing the evidence, the writ petition attacked the inquiry's procedure itself — notice, opportunity, and a finding recorded without material.
Result
Dismissal quashed; reinstatement directed with consequential benefits, seniority and continuity of service intact.
CriminalAcquittal on appeal

A conviction undone by its own record.

Challenge
A trial-court conviction resting on eyewitness testimony that had hardened considerably between the FIR and the deposition.
Approach
The appeal was built on the contradictions chart — statement against statement, date against date — until the improvements could not be explained away.
Result
Conviction set aside in appeal; the appellant acquitted and released.
PropertyTitle protected

Two decades of litigation, one clear title.

Challenge
A family's ancestral land clouded by competing sale deeds and a rival claim pressed through successive suits and appeals.
Approach
The revenue record was reconstructed entry by entry, and the interim injunction defended at every tier while the title suit was fought to judgment.
Result
Title declared and possession protected; the decree sustained through appeal.
